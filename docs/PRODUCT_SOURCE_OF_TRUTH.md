# MIKAYLA product source of truth

## Product

MIKAYLA is an AI-powered travel fashion intelligence platform. It reads the visual language of a city, translates that language into complete outfits for a traveller’s real itinerary, prioritises clothes the traveller already owns, and makes genuine wardrobe gaps shoppable at several price points.

## Core promise

Every city has its own dress code. MIKAYLA reads it.

The user outcome is confidence: fit in while standing out.

## Launch audience

MIKAYLA is for anyone travelling to an unfamiliar city, from a first-time
traveller to a frequent traveller. The product must not assume specialist
fashion knowledge, an established travel routine, or confidence interpreting
local style. It should become the traveller's trusted resource for accurate,
current city trends and attire suited to the specific places on their
itinerary.

The launch fashion catalogue is primarily women's fashion. Users should first
be allowed to see the full relevant selection, then optionally refine it by
colour, clothing size, shoe size, fit, and other preferences. Men's fashion is
a possible later expansion and must not dilute the quality or clarity of the
women's launch experience.

MIKAYLA should support the broadest reliable range of standard, petite, tall,
plus-size, maternity, adaptive, and wide-fit products available through its
approved catalogues. It must not imply that an original inspiration item is
available in every size. When it is not, the system should preserve the
silhouette, colour story, proportions, material impression, and styling effect
as closely as possible in a size-compatible alternative.

The launch commerce experience serves travellers shopping from Canada, the
United States, the United Kingdom, Europe, and Australia. Recommendations must
respect the customer's market, currency, regional sizing, shipping eligibility,
season, and product availability rather than assuming a US-only catalogue.

## Intended experience

1. Open with a carefully curated, Pinterest-like editorial gallery that
   combines luxury-campaign polish with aspirational travel imagery.
2. Let the traveller search any destination immediately.
3. Return current, source-linked visual references from approved creator,
   editorial, Pinterest, TikTok, and other licensed or API-accessible sources.
4. Make each useful look shoppable, with visually coherent alternatives at
   luxury, contemporary, and accessible price levels.
5. After the traveller understands the city's overall aesthetic, invite them to
   upload or paste an itinerary.
6. Interpret each restaurant, landmark, and occasion using available public
   evidence such as approved geotagged social references, venue imagery, and
   Google place or review data; then curate complete, shoppable outfits.
7. Let the traveller upload their existing wardrobe. Recognised items become
   owned pieces that should be reused before recommending a purchase.
8. Bring owned and recommended pieces into a Clueless-inspired studio where
   tops, bottoms, shoes, bags, hats, and other accessories can be mixed,
   matched, saved, and assigned to itinerary events.

The discovery journey should feel progressive: inspiration first, contextual
planning second, personal wardrobe intelligence third.

## Discovery and recommendation hierarchy

Initial results should be ordered by the strongest trustworthy signals of what
is currently trending and, when verified commerce data exists, what travellers
or relevant customers are purchasing. A recommendation must never be labelled
popular or frequently purchased without a real supporting signal.

The first presentation should optimise for the closest city aesthetic, fit, and
occasion—not the lowest price. Results can then be refined using:

- live destination weather
- neighbourhood or venue location
- occasion and dress code
- an optional outfit budget
- the traveller's owned wardrobe and saved preferences

The contemporary or mid-market interpretation is the default and should appear
first, using retailers in the general world of Revolve where catalogue access,
regional availability, and product relevance permit it. A more accessible and
a more luxurious interpretation should be available as understated
alternatives.

Price is supporting information. It should remain clear and honest, but subtle
and tasteful so the customer encounters the image, relevance, and overall
aesthetic before cost. Users may optionally set a total outfit budget without
turning the experience into a price-comparison interface.

## Recommendation evidence

Every externally sourced inspiration item should retain:

- creator or publisher
- original platform or source
- city or venue relevance
- publication or freshness information when available
- a concise reason it was recommended
- a working route to the original source

Commerce results should also retain the retailer, current price and currency,
availability information when supplied by the retailer, and the distinction
between an exact item and a stylistically similar alternative.

Size availability is part of product relevance. An unavailable exact item may
still be identified for transparency, but the first actionable recommendation
should be purchasable in the user's selected size whenever the catalogue
provides reliable size data.

Creator looks may surface established retailers, niche designers, independent
labels, or boutiques. A retailer's size or familiarity must not exclude it when
the product, product page, fulfilment destination, and source evidence can be
verified.

An "exact" match means the same identifiable product shown in the inspiration,
supported by sufficient visual or source evidence and linked to the
corresponding product page. All other results must be labelled as similar,
inspired by, or an alternative. MIKAYLA must never use a visually convenient
substitute to claim an exact identification.

## Inspiration media rights

Public availability is not treated as permission to copy or redistribute a
creator's work. MIKAYLA should display third-party inspiration through:

- an official platform embed that preserves the platform experience,
  attribution, and original-post link
- a platform API or licensed provider whose terms permit the intended display
- direct written creator, publisher, agency, or rights-holder permission
- properly licensed editorial or commercial media

MIKAYLA should not download, crop, remove attribution from, or rehost creator
photography merely because it appears publicly on Instagram, Pinterest, TikTok,
or another site. Platform-specific terms must be reviewed before each
integration is enabled.

Every creator item requires a visible creator name, platform or publisher,
original source link, and a removal or rights-contact route. Removed, private,
expired, or unavailable source posts should disappear from the live feed. A
formal rights and takedown workflow plus qualified legal review are launch
requirements for the creator feed.

## Accounts and saved intelligence

City search and initial inspiration remain open without an account. Registration
is introduced only after the traveller has experienced enough value to want to
keep something.

Account creation remains optional; users are never required to register merely
to search, browse, or follow an outbound shopping link. Email, Google, and Apple
may be offered as convenient sign-up methods. When registration begins from a
save action, the intended save must survive the sign-up flow.

An account enables the traveller to save and reconnect:

- inspiration photographs and source posts
- private inspiration boards for individual destinations or trips
- followed creators and preferred inspiration sources
- city searches and selected aesthetics
- itineraries and individual itinerary events
- recommended and purchased products
- recognised closet pieces
- outfits assembled in the studio
- saved looks assigned to specific trip dates and occasions

The account experience should feel like a private travel-fashion memory, not an
administrative dashboard. Saved inspiration, owned pieces, generated outfits,
and itinerary context must remain linked so the product becomes more useful
over time.

Users can love, save, hide, or mark a look as "not my style." These signals
should gradually personalise ranking, silhouettes, colour direction, creator
references, and outfit variety without forcing a style quiz before the user can
explore.

Saved content is private by default. A user may deliberately share a private
board or outfit with friends through a controlled link.

Users may also opt into a public profile and explicitly publish selected boards.
Other users can browse those public boards for inspiration and collaborate
where the board owner permits it. Creating a public profile must not
automatically expose saved searches, itineraries, dates, bookings, closet
photos, purchase state, private boards, or location information.

Public profiles can be followed. Other users can save a published board, like
an outfit, and request or accept an invitation to collaborate on a board. Board
owners control collaborators and can revoke access. Public comments and direct
messages are outside the launch scope so that inspiration and collaboration do
not turn into an unmoderated social network.

Community boards belong in a clearly identified Community Inspiration area.
They must not be mixed into MIKAYLA's verified city feed or used as evidence of
a local trend merely because another user published them.

## Private inspiration vault

Original closet photographs, outfit references, and uploaded itinerary images
or documents are retained in a private account vault so the user can return to
the original styling, accessories, proportions, and context. AI-extracted
garments, events, and looks remain connected to their source upload.

Vault media is private by default and must not appear on a public profile or
board unless the user deliberately publishes a specific eligible item. The
user can download or permanently delete original uploads and related extracted
data. Private media should use encrypted transport, private object storage,
short-lived access links, and strict account-level authorisation. The product
must clearly explain retention before upload.

## Reminders

Registered users may opt into useful email reminders for saved trips and
shopping activity. Early reminders can include packing deadlines, material
weather changes, saved-product availability, and meaningful price changes.
Order and delivery reminders can be added when authorised commerce integrations
exist. Mobile push notifications belong to the later native-app phase.

Operational trip reminders and marketing email consent must remain separate.
Users control reminder categories, frequency, and unsubscribe settings.

## Commerce interaction

Selecting a recommended item opens an elegant in-site quick view before the
customer leaves MIKAYLA. It should show the product imagery, retailer, current
price and currency, relevant size or availability information, why the piece
matches the city or outfit, and closely related price-tier alternatives. The
primary purchase action then opens the verified retailer product page.

Affiliate tracking can be introduced later by replacing eligible outbound
retailer links after program approval. Monetisation must never reduce visual
quality, recommendation relevance, or link accuracy.

The customer product is free. City discovery, itinerary styling, closet tools,
saved inspiration, trip planning, packing tools, and the outfit studio should
not be separated into paid subscription tiers. MIKAYLA's primary business model
is disclosed affiliate commission earned when customers purchase eligible
products through outbound links.

Affiliate commission rate must never determine recommendation rank. The best
city, occasion, weather, taste, fit, regional availability, and wardrobe match
appears first even when another eligible item would pay MIKAYLA more.

The longer-term commerce vision includes brand partnerships, travel
partnerships, streamlined or on-site checkout where agreements permit it, and a
unified delivery view that tracks orders placed across multiple retailers.
Order status and tracking numbers must come from authorised retailer, carrier,
commerce-platform, or user-consented email integrations. MIKAYLA must never
invent fulfilment state or imply that an external order was placed through
MIKAYLA when it was not.

The following are release-blocking commerce failures:

- generic Unsplash or unrelated stock imagery presented as shoppable fashion
- a product image that does not correspond to the linked item
- a link that opens a retailer search when a verified product page is promised
- alternatives that share a price tier but not the silhouette or aesthetic
- unavailable, region-incompatible, or stale items shown without a clear status
- affiliate placement influencing the recommendation without disclosure

SHEIN, Fashion Nova, and similarly positioned low-trust ultra-fast-fashion
marketplaces are excluded from recommendations. The exclusion list can expand
as MIKAYLA establishes retailer quality, image accuracy, fulfilment, and brand
standards.

## Primary journeys

1. Discover current city style through sourced creator and editorial references.
2. Upload a look and identify exact or similar pieces at luxury, contemporary, and accessible price points.
3. Upload or paste an itinerary and receive venue-aware outfits for every event.
4. Build a private digital closet and avoid duplicate recommendations.
5. Mix tops, bottoms, shoes, bags, and accessories in a Clueless-style studio, then assign saved looks to dates.

## Product principles

- Local authenticity over editorial fantasy.
- Personal first, purchase second.
- Timeless enough to rewear, current enough to feel relevant.
- Budget translation without aesthetic judgement.
- Aesthetic and occasion fit before price comparison.
- Contemporary interpretation first; accessible and luxury alternatives remain
  close at hand.
- Complete looks include accessories.
- City and venue context matter more than generic global trends.
- Body and fit preferences are user-controlled.
- Public source evidence and commercial relationships are visible.
- Let the customer experience value before requesting registration.
- Saving turns inspiration, itinerary, closet, and outfits into one connected
  personal archive.

## Data honesty

Live social intelligence requires approved API or licensed-provider access. The product must distinguish:

- live configured data
- curated public references
- editorial interpretation
- fallback or demo output

No public-facing screen may imply private-data access, invented engagement, invented product availability, or fabricated confidence.

## Brand

Chic. Effortless. Timeless. Elegant. Confident. Quiet. Knowing.

The experience should feel like a luxury fashion house made a genuinely useful travel tool.

The visual system is editorial, minimal, and predominantly black and cream.
Imagery is a deliberate mixture of luxury campaigns and travel editorials. The
site must not use generic stock splash photography to fill space. Every
customer-facing image should be carefully selected because it is genuinely
inspirational, relevant to the city, and useful as a path into shopping or
styling.

The interface should keep the imagery aspirational while making the product
easy for a first-time traveller to understand. Working links, visible source
credit, meaningful affordability choices, and accurate retailer destinations
are part of the brand promise, not merely technical details.

The name itself remains the primary identity. MIKAYLA uses a proprietary-feeling
high-contrast couture serif wordmark with elongated proportions, a larger
opening M, and a restrained curved flourish extending from the K. The reference
feeling is an established editorial fashion house rather than a racing brand.
The final production wordmark should eventually be redrawn and licensed as
custom lettering so its forms belong to MIKAYLA alone.

Standalone emblem development is paused. For the current website, the MIKAYLA
wordmark is the sole visible brand identity, using the exact couture-serif
lettering direction supplied by the founder. Earlier emblem studies remain
exploratory only and are not active brand marks.

The identity remains black and warm cream. Before broad commercial rollout,
the custom wordmark requires professional vector refinement, font licensing
confirmation, and trademark clearance.

MIKAYLA has two complementary expressions:

- a quiet, authoritative editorial presence in city discovery and visual
  merchandising
- a warm but professional AI stylist inside itinerary, closet, and outfit
  planning

The AI must never feel casual, sloppy, overly chatty, or uncertain about an
unsupported claim. Its recommendations are concise, considered, and clearly
reasoned. The complete experience should earn enough trust that travellers
return for every trip, whether a weekend away or a long itinerary.

MIKAYLA is a fashion inspiration and identification product. It should not add
political, religious, moral, or general lifestyle commentary to styling
results. Weather and explicit venue dress codes may inform recommendations only
when directly relevant to the user's selected occasion; they should appear as
concise styling context rather than warnings or lectures.

## Initial city set

Paris, New York, London, Tokyo, Milan, Barcelona, Dubai, Amsterdam, Rome, Santorini, Amalfi Coast, Copenhagen, Lisbon, Mykonos, Tulum, Cape Town, Bali, Vienna, Prague, and Edinburgh.

City search is not restricted to this set. Every destination should be
supported, with the richest depth reserved for destinations that have the
strongest trustworthy evidence and traveller demand. High-volume seasonal
destinations—especially popular European summer cities and coastal regions—
should naturally have more imagery, creators, looks, venue intelligence, and
shopping options.

The first editorial depth programme prioritises major European fashion and
summer destinations, treating nearby places as distinct aesthetics rather than
generic country-level feeds. The priority group begins with Paris, London,
Milan, Positano and the Amalfi Coast, Mykonos, Paros, Rome, Barcelona, Lisbon,
Ibiza, Saint-Tropez, Cannes, Copenhagen, and Dubai. This list is a merchandising
priority, not a limit on search coverage, and can shift with season and demand.

Result depth must never be confused with certainty. Each result should be
labelled where necessary as live sourced evidence, current editorial reference,
curated historical context, or MIKAYLA interpretation.

## Itinerary wardrobe output

Every named occasion, restaurant, landmark, neighbourhood, or event should
receive several complete outfit options rather than a single prescriptive
answer. Options should be meaningfully different in silhouette, styling
attitude, practicality, or personal taste while remaining appropriate to the
same context.

The initial presentation for each occasion contains three distinct directions:

1. understated
2. fashion-forward
3. practical

These are differences in styling attitude, not quality or price tiers. Each
direction remains complete, current, venue-appropriate, weather-aware, and
shoppable. The ranking within and across directions becomes more personal as
MIKAYLA learns from saves, loves, hides, and "not my style" feedback.

The itinerary experience may also generate, at the traveller's choice:

- a visual trip calendar
- a capsule wardrobe
- a complete packing list
- last-minute or low-effort recommendations
- day-to-evening outfit changes
- live weather adjustments
- a summary of pieces reused across the trip
- a shopping list containing only genuine wardrobe gaps

Users can set how many times they are willing to rewear an individual article
of clothing. The planner should reuse strong pieces within that limit and show
the effect on the capsule and packing list.

These tools should be especially reassuring for last-minute and less organised
travellers who do not know where to begin. The product should turn an
unstructured itinerary into a clear, attainable wardrobe plan without making
the traveller feel behind.

Itineraries can begin as pasted text, screenshots, PDFs, booking confirmations,
calendar imports, or forwarded travel emails. The first launch workflow
prioritises pasted text, screenshots, and PDFs. AI extraction must always
produce an editable trip timeline so the traveller can confirm dates, times,
venues, cities, and occasion types before outfit generation.

## Current implementation strategy

- Vercel hosts the web application and serverless endpoints.
- GitHub is the source of record.
- The interface must remain useful without paid API credentials through clearly labelled local fallbacks.
- Anthropic vision and text models provide image and itinerary interpretation when configured.
- Pinterest Trends supplies current search-growth signals when approved access is configured.
- A licensed image-search provider supplies current, source-linked public Pinterest, Instagram, and TikTok post discovery for each city without requiring daily board maintenance. Search rank is a discovery signal; a trend claim still requires recency and repeated evidence.
- The MIKAYLA Pinterest account is an outbound brand, editorial, and future affiliate-publishing channel. It is not an input to the website's city-trend engine.
- The Pins saved during product development are visual-direction references only.
- The current release uses direct retailer searches at luxury, contemporary, and accessible price levels.
- Approved affiliate-network or retailer feeds can later supply product details and trackable links without changing the interface.
- Closet and trip data remain local unless secure account storage is configured.
- Closet ingestion begins with individual and batch photographs. AI may detect
  and separate multiple garments, but the traveller confirms category, colour,
  size, and brand before the items become trusted closet records.
