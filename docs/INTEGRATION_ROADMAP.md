# MIKAYLA integration roadmap

## Active product model

- The opening experience is a luxury editorial introduction built around a
  curated, Pinterest-like gallery and an immediate destination search.
- Pinterest and creator imagery belongs inside city discovery, where every visual keeps its source credit.
- Approved creator, editorial, Pinterest, TikTok, venue, geotag, and review
  sources should contribute to the product when their APIs or licensed data
  access permit it.
- Shopping actions must resolve to working retailer destinations across luxury,
  contemporary, and accessible price levels. Direct search results are an
  interim mechanism, not the desired final commerce experience.
- The mid-market interpretation appears first. Price and alternative tiers are
  present but visually secondary to the outfit, city fit, and styling rationale.
- Default ranking uses current trend signals and verified purchase signals when
  available, then adapts to live weather, location, occasion, optional outfit
  budget, and owned pieces.
- Search supports all cities. Evidence-rich, high-demand destinations receive
  deeper results, while lower-signal destinations use clearly labelled
  editorial interpretation instead of fabricated volume.
- The product does not claim an affiliate relationship or commission while no active program is configured.
- Closet, itinerary, and studio data stay on the user's device until secure accounts and storage are added.
- Generic stock imagery is not an acceptable fallback. If a trustworthy and
  aesthetically suitable visual is unavailable, the interface should use
  typography, layout, or an honest empty state instead.
- Anonymous visitors can search and explore. Account creation becomes necessary
  only when saving inspiration, searches, itineraries, closet pieces, or studio
  outfits.
- All customer-facing product capabilities remain free. Revenue comes from
  disclosed affiliate commissions and later approved commercial partnerships,
  not a consumer subscription.
- Launch commerce localisation covers Canada, the United States, the United
  Kingdom, Europe, and Australia.
- Launch should be comprehensive enough for a traveller to complete the full
  discovery-to-itinerary-to-closet-to-outfit journey without encountering a
  patchwork of visibly unfinished modules. Partner-dependent capabilities must
  have an honest, useful launch state rather than a deceptive simulation.

## Launch approach

1. Begin with a private beta of approximately 25–50 travellers to test source
   quality, outfit relevance, link accuracy, itinerary extraction, closet
   recognition, and mobile usability.
2. Resolve systemic trust failures before public launch; do not postpone broken
   links, incorrect product-image matches, misleading source labels, or data
   loss as minor future tweaks.
3. Publish a comprehensive web experience first. Add native-app push,
   cross-retailer delivery tracking, unified checkout, and broad partner
   automation as later platform phases.
4. Use MIKAYLA itself as the sole active high-contrast couture-serif wordmark.
   Defer all standalone emblem development until the founder returns to it.
5. Commission professional vector refinement, final custom lettering, font
   licensing review, and trademark clearance before broad commercial rollout.

## Owned channels to connect

1. Use approved Pinterest Trends access for current regional fashion keywords.
2. Use a licensed image-search provider for automatically refreshed, source-linked public Pinterest, Instagram, and TikTok posts by city. Render or link them through platform-supported presentation rather than copying them into MIKAYLA-owned media storage.
3. Use the MIKAYLA Pinterest Business account to publish branded editorial Pins and, only after approval, disclosed affiliate Pins that drive traffic back to mikaylaco.com.
4. Treat development mood-board Pins as visual direction, never as city-trend evidence.
5. Add MIKAYLA's Instagram profile link as an owned social channel.
6. Use approved Pinterest, TikTok, Meta, Google, or licensed-provider access for
   live signal ingestion; do not scrape private or restricted social content.
7. Use venue and review evidence to infer occasion context only when the source
   terms permit it. Distinguish observed facts from MIKAYLA's editorial styling
   interpretation.
8. Keep source credit, freshness, and fallback labels visible whenever live signals are shown.
9. Add a weather provider with current conditions and forecast coverage for the
   traveller's dates; show when weather was last refreshed.
10. Use official platform embeds, approved APIs, licensed-provider display
    rights, or direct rights-holder permission for creator media. Do not
    download and rehost public posts as a substitute for permission.
11. Add creator rights contact, takedown handling, source-availability checks,
    and qualified legal review before enabling the creator feed publicly.

## Commerce progression

1. Begin with useful direct retailer searches.
2. Add verified retailer catalogue feeds for product title, image, live price,
   availability, sizing, destination, and exact-versus-similar matching.
3. Apply to affiliate programs after the product has active content and audience traction.
4. Turn on tracked links and the corresponding disclosure only after a relationship is approved and configured.
5. Validate outbound links and exclude stale or unavailable products so that
   affordability choices remain genuinely purchasable.
6. Prefer a contemporary catalogue result first, including relevant Revolve
   inventory when an approved feed or retailer integration is available; group
   more accessible and luxury translations behind the same look.
7. Use product-level purchase counts only when supplied by an authorised,
   auditable source. Otherwise rank with available trend, relevance, and
   editorial signals without implying sales volume.
8. Replace the interim search-link experience with a MIKAYLA quick view backed
   by verified product records and a direct outbound retailer product URL.
9. Require product-image-to-link consistency, aesthetic similarity, region
   compatibility, and availability checks before enabling affiliate tracking.
10. Introduce affiliate parameters and disclosures only after each retailer or
    network relationship is approved.
11. Keep affiliate commission entirely out of relevance ranking. Store the
    commercial relationship separately from the styling score and preserve an
    auditable explanation of why each product was selected.
12. Localise currency, retailer catalogue, regional sizing, shipping
    eligibility, returns information, and seasonal context for Canada, the US,
    UK, Europe, and Australia.
13. Add brand and travel partnerships only when their placement and commercial
    relationship can be disclosed without changing recommendation integrity.
14. Explore streamlined or on-site checkout through approved retailer and
    commerce-platform relationships.
15. Build a unified delivery view using authorised retailer webhooks, carrier
    APIs, commerce-platform connections, or explicit user email access. Keep
    externally completed purchases distinct from orders processed by MIKAYLA.
16. Maintain a retailer exclusion policy beginning with SHEIN, Fashion Nova,
    and comparable low-trust ultra-fast-fashion marketplaces.
17. Treat selected-size availability as ranking input. When an exact item is not
    size-compatible, preserve the inspiration's visual attributes in the closest
    verified alternative rather than presenting an unrelated substitute.
18. Include verified niche designers, independent labels, and boutiques
    discovered through creator looks; label unverified or non-identical matches
    as similar rather than exact.

## Account progression

1. Keep city discovery and initial shopping inspiration available anonymously.
2. Prompt for an account at the first save action, preserving the item the user
   intended to save through registration.
3. Model saved inspiration, searches, trips, itinerary events, closet pieces,
   studio looks, and purchase state as related account data.
4. Add cross-device sync and a private saved collection when secure account
   storage is configured.
5. Keep the launch catalogue focused on women's fashion, with optional
   refinements for colour, sizing, fit, and other preferences. Treat men's
   fashion as a later catalogue and product expansion.
6. Support standard, petite, tall, plus-size, maternity, adaptive, and wide-fit
   inventory whenever approved catalogues provide reliable attributes; never
   fabricate size coverage.
7. Add private destination and trip boards, followed creators, and love, save,
   hide, and "not my style" feedback.
8. Use explicit feedback to personalise ranking while keeping the customer's
   feedback history editable and avoiding opaque or irreversible taste labels.
9. Keep all saved content private by default and add intentional link sharing
   for selected boards or looks.
10. Add opt-in public profiles and explicitly published public boards without
    exposing itineraries, dates, bookings, closet photos, purchase state, or
    location data.
11. Add board collaboration with owner-controlled permissions and clear removal
    of collaborators.
12. Add profile following, public-board saving, and outfit likes. Keep public
    comments and direct messages outside the launch scope.
13. Isolate Community Inspiration from MIKAYLA's verified editorial city feed
    and do not treat community publication as trend evidence.
14. Offer email, Google, and Apple sign-in without requiring an account for
    browsing, search, or outbound shopping.

## Private media progression

1. Store original closet photographs, outfit references, and itinerary uploads
   in a private account vault linked to extracted garments, events, and looks.
2. Use private object storage, encrypted transport, short-lived signed access,
   and account-level authorisation rather than public media URLs.
3. Keep vaulted uploads off public profiles and boards unless the user makes a
   deliberate item-level publication choice.
4. Add download and permanent deletion controls for originals and related
   extracted records, with clear retention language before upload.

## Reminder progression

1. Begin with opt-in email reminders for packing deadlines, important weather
   changes, saved-item availability, and meaningful price changes.
2. Add order and delivery email updates only after authorised commerce data is
   available.
3. Keep transactional trip reminders separate from marketing consent and give
   users category and frequency controls.
4. Add push notifications during the native-app phase, not as a web-launch
   dependency.

## Editorial destination progression

1. Maintain universal city search while investing the deepest launch coverage
   in major European fashion and summer destinations.
2. Begin with Paris, London, Milan, Positano and the Amalfi Coast, Mykonos,
   Paros, Rome, Barcelona, Lisbon, Ibiza, Saint-Tropez, Cannes, Copenhagen, and
   Dubai.
3. Build separate destination taxonomies for islands, coastal towns, cities,
   and neighbourhoods rather than flattening Greece, Italy, or other countries
   into one aesthetic.
4. Rebalance editorial depth seasonally using trustworthy traveller demand and
   source availability.

## Trip-planning progression

1. Produce several complete looks for every itinerary event rather than a single
   answer.
2. Present three initial styling directions for each event: understated,
   fashion-forward, and practical. Keep these independent from affordability
   tiers.
3. Add a visual trip calendar, capsule wardrobe, packing list, wardrobe-gap
   list, day-to-evening changes, and last-minute planning mode.
4. Let users set per-item or default rewear limits and optimise the capsule
   around those limits.
5. Recalculate recommendations when weather, event details, owned pieces, saved
   taste, or purchase state changes.
6. Preserve the relationship between every look, its itinerary event, its
   source inspiration, and its owned or purchasable pieces.
7. Launch itinerary ingestion with pasted text, screenshots, and PDFs; add
   calendar imports, booking connections, and forwarded email ingestion later.
8. Require an editable timeline confirmation step before generating outfits
   from extracted itinerary data.

## Closet progression

1. Support individual and batch wardrobe photographs first.
2. Detect and separate garments, then require a lightweight user confirmation
   of category, colour, size, and brand.
3. Preserve confidence and correction history so uncertain AI identification
   does not silently become trusted wardrobe data.
4. Add receipt, order-email, or wardrobe-video ingestion only after the core
   image workflow is accurate and secure.
